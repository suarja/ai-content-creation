# Short-video captions

A caption has two jobs: tell the search box and the scroller what the video is about, and ask
for one action. Everything else (sources, the app pitch, the link) goes in the pinned comment.

A week of captions written in one mould (hook, emoji, a list of three behind a colon, a maxim, a
question) was rejected at once: the mould itself is an AI tell. This skill exists to avoid it.

## Before writing

1. If a copywriting skill with an AI-tells list is installed, load it: its blacklist applies to
   captions, and its self-check runs on every caption before hand-over.
2. Watch the video or read its composition: list the exact words and numbers on screen. The
   caption reuses them; it never adds a number the video does not show.
3. Read the account's own best captions, ranked by views. A strong model is short, concrete and
   asks one action: "Boost your ChatGPT with this custom agent 🦾 comment "PROMPT" to get it".

## The shape

- **Line 1, at most about 90 characters**: what the viewer sees in the first second, in the words
  someone would type in search ("TikTok hooks", "TikTok algorithm", "video editing", the niche).
  A concrete scene or the video's real numbers beat a promise. One emoji, at the end of this line,
  or none.
- **Line 2, one sentence**: the single action. Save it, try one thing tonight, answer in a word in
  the comments, or look at one precise thing. One action, never two.
- **Hashtags**: three to five, topic first, then niche, then the brand tag. Instagram caps posts at
  five.
- Total under about 200 characters before the hashtags. Instagram cuts around 125.

## Banned in captions

- A list of three behind a colon: "Hook, promise, payoff: save it".
- A maxim or moral: "Editing can't save a slow start", "it all comes down to your hook".
- "Guess…", "You won't believe…", "Wait for the end", and other bait the video does not pay off.
- A sentence that adds clauses after its claim, with commas ("…, it all comes down to your hook").
- Two questions, or a question followed by its own answer.
- The em dash, exclamation marks, more than one emoji.
- "I" when the author did not do it. A POV scenario stays in "you".
- The app pitch in the caption. It lives in the pinned comment.

## The pinned comment

One or two sentences: the source if the video makes a factual claim (title and short link), then
the app line, "<Mascot or app> [does the thing] in <App>, link in bio 👆". The promise must be
true in the shipped app; check the code, not only its strings.

## Other languages

Same shape in every language. Write each caption from the video, never by translating another
language's caption: search words differ ("algo TikTok" in French is "TikTok algorithm" in
English). Use the language's own number format (80,412 in English, 80 412 in French).

## Hand-over

Write the caption and the pinned comment in the video's publication file, in fenced blocks so
they copy cleanly. If the caption is already typed in a TikTok Studio draft, say so and replace it
there too: a draft keeps its old text until someone edits it.
